# Sprint 5 --- Quality Control, Minimum-N Analysis & Low-N Diagnostics

## 1. Purpose of Sprint 5

Sprint 5 establishes the **quality-control layer** of OpenETBench.

The benchmark does not contain the same number of observations for every
site--product combination. Differences arise from product temporal
resolution, data availability, missing observations, and the temporal
overlap between a product and the BharatFlux observations.

Therefore, a benchmark result should not be interpreted only from its
error metrics. We also need to know **how much observational evidence
supports each result**.

Sprint 5 addresses this by:

-   applying a minimum observation threshold;
-   categorizing combinations according to their available sample size
    `N`;
-   separating statistically/temporally usable combinations from
    low-sample cases;
-   producing a primary benchmark containing only combinations meeting
    the minimum `N`;
-   diagnosing the low-`N` combinations instead of silently discarding
    them.

The default threshold used in this sprint was:

``` text
Minimum N = 10
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. What is N?

`N` is the **number of matched, valid observations** available for a
site--product comparison after the extraction and temporal alignment
steps.

For example:

-   `N = 366` means 366 valid matched observations;
-   `N = 46` may correspond to an approximately 8-day product;
-   `N = 12` may correspond to approximately monthly observations;
-   `N = 2` or `N = 3` provides very little evidence for judging a
    product.

This matters because error metrics computed from a very small number of
observations can be unstable and misleading.

Thus, Sprint 5 does not treat all benchmark rows as equally reliable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. How Sprint 5 was accomplished

The Sprint 5 workflow operates on the consolidated multi-site benchmark
generated by Sprint 4.

### Step 1 --- Read the consolidated benchmark

The input was:

``` text
results/summary/multisite_benchmark.csv
```

This contains the site--product combinations and their:

-   `N`
-   RMSE
-   MAE
-   Bias
-   Correlation
-   R²
-   temporal coverage information

### Step 2 --- Categorize N

Each combination was classified into an `N_CATEGORY`:

  Category       Interpretation
  -------------- -------------------------------------------------
  INSUFFICIENT   Very small observational support
  LIMITED        Low observational support
  ADEQUATE       Acceptable sample size for the primary analysis
  STRONG         Strong observational support

The classification is intended as a **quality-control diagnostic**, not
as a claim that a particular numerical `N` universally guarantees
statistical validity.

### Step 3 --- Apply the primary minimum-N threshold

For this benchmark:

``` text
N >= 10
```

was selected as the primary eligibility criterion.

Combinations with `N < 10` were retained separately as low-`N` cases
rather than being deleted.

### Step 4 --- Produce QC outputs

Sprint 5 generated:

``` text
results/summary/sprint5/qc_benchmark.csv
results/summary/sprint5/qc_filtered_benchmark.csv
results/summary/sprint5/product_summary.csv
results/summary/sprint5/site_summary.csv
results/summary/sprint5/n_distribution.csv
results/summary/sprint5/qc_report.json
```

### Step 5 --- Perform the low-N diagnostic

Sprint 5.5 further examined the combinations with `N < 10`.

The diagnostic considers the expected temporal sampling of the product
so that low `N` is not automatically interpreted as a data-quality
failure.

For example, an 8-day product naturally produces fewer observations than
a daily product, while a monthly/irregular product may produce even
fewer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Sprint 5 Results

The consolidated Sprint 4 benchmark contained:

``` text
Successful site–product combinations : 64
Minimum N                            : 10
Primary combinations (N >= 10)      : 55
Low-N combinations (N < 10)         : 9
Eligibility rate                    : 85.94%
Sprint 4 logged failures             : 2
```

### N distribution

  N category       Combinations   Percentage
  -------------- -------------- ------------
  INSUFFICIENT                4        6.25%
  LIMITED                     5        7.81%
  ADEQUATE                    9       14.06%
  STRONG                     46       71.88%
  **Total**              **64**     **100%**

The key result is that **71.88% of successful combinations have strong
observational support**, while **85.94% meet the primary `N >= 10`
criterion**.

Therefore, the benchmark retains a substantial primary analysis set
without discarding the lower-sample cases from the project entirely.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Low-N findings

Sprint 5.5 identified 9 low-`N` combinations.

The low-`N` cases are concentrated in products with relatively sparse
temporal sampling:

-   **FLDAS:** 5 low-`N` combinations
-   **MOD16A2GF:** 2 low-`N` combinations
-   **PMLV2:** 2 low-`N` combinations

The affected sites were concentrated around:

-   BKC
-   SIT
-   BIT
-   JIT
-   NIT
-   SFT

The diagnostic showed that low `N` is often explainable by the product's
temporal sampling rather than automatically indicating extraction
failure.

Examples include:

-   MOD16A2GF --- approximately 8-day sampling;
-   PMLV2 --- approximately 8-day sampling;
-   FLDAS --- sparse/monthly or irregular temporal sampling in the
    extracted benchmark.

This distinction is important: **a low `N` should be interpreted in the
context of the product's native temporal resolution and actual temporal
overlap with the reference observations.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Interpretation

### 6.1 The benchmark has sufficient primary coverage

With 55 of 64 successful combinations satisfying `N >= 10`, the
benchmark has a sufficiently large primary subset for cross-product
interpretation.

The 85.94% eligibility rate is a positive result for the current
dataset.

### 6.2 Low-N results should not be ranked equally with primary results

A result based on `N = 2` or `N = 3` should not carry the same
evidential weight as one based on `N = 210` or `N = 366`.

Sprint 5 therefore establishes a clean separation:

``` text
Primary benchmark  → N >= 10
Low-N diagnostic    → N < 10
```

### 6.3 Low N is partly a product-characteristic issue

The low-N diagnostic shows that some low sample counts are expected from
the temporal resolution of the products.

Consequently, OpenETBench should **not interpret low N alone as evidence
that a product performs poorly**.

### 6.4 Sprint 5 provides the statistical foundation for Sprint 6

The product-level interpretation in Sprint 6 is based on the **primary
benchmark**, rather than mixing highly reliable and extremely sparse
comparisons.

This makes the subsequent ranking and cross-site interpretation more
defensible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Key Sprint 5 conclusions

1.  **N is a critical quality-control variable** because it measures the
    amount of matched observational evidence behind every benchmark
    result.
2.  **85.94% of successful combinations satisfy `N >= 10`.**
3.  **71.88% have strong sample support** according to the Sprint 5
    categorization.
4.  The 9 low-N cases are concentrated in **FLDAS, MOD16A2GF and
    PMLV2**.
5.  Low N is frequently associated with **sparse/native temporal
    sampling and temporal overlap**, rather than necessarily indicating
    a pipeline failure.
6.  Low-N combinations are retained for diagnostic purposes but excluded
    from the primary benchmark interpretation.
7.  Sprint 5 therefore establishes the **quality-controlled primary
    dataset used by Sprint 6**.
